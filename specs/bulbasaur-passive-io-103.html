Bulbasaur passive disk timing

Status: planned diagnostic, not a verified repair. Issue 103, CONTROLPLANE_API_STALL and METAL_STORAGE_PATH. Prior observations on November 1, 2026 showed root-disk saturation and approximately two-second write/flush latency. Attribution and hardware cause remain unknown.

Scope and prerequisites

The repository-owned script binds root SSH execution to Bulbasaur’s machine ID, root UUID, disk size, device number and Fedora 44. SELinux must remain enforcing. It observes existing sda I/O for 1,800 seconds. No synthetic load, filesystem writes to production data, disk tuning, package installation, service restart, scheduling change or NAS operation is included.

Only bpf-biosnoop is extracted from the exact Fedora-signed RPM into a private disposable native directory. The RPM SHA256, full Fedora 44 signing fingerprint, NEVRA and signed per-file digest are checked. Existing native libraries are used. The executable is removed after the owned tracer exits.

Measurement and interpretation

Queue and service latency are collected separately without a service-latency filter. Every output record is parsed, event losses and unexpected stderr make coverage incomplete, and compact per-minute/process/operation aggregates are retained. Disk statistics and I/O pressure are sampled every ten seconds. UTC timestamps are userspace completion receipt time.

Upstream attributes requests to the last merged BIO process. Requeues and partial completions limit attribution and timing. A quiet window does not resolve earlier stalls, and timings alone do not establish a hardware failure or an error count. A slow queue and a slow device service time require different follow-up; no tuning is authorized merely by this capture.

Cleanup and rollback

The tracked parent reaps only its tracer and extraction children. Ordinary failure and signals trigger cleanup of its exact native scratch directory. Closing the tracer removes its unpinned BPF objects; no global BPF cleanup touches Cilium. No durable host setting changes, so rollback consists of stopping the owned tracer and removing its scratch.
